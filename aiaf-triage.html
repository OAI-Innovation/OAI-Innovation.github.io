AIAF triage

Start with this quick check. Answer three questions to determine whether your use case must complete the AIAF.

Start with the use case

An AIAF assessment considers a specific way AI will be used by an agency—not the underlying platform, product or tool. Platforms and products are considered through procurement, cyber security, privacy and technical assurance and are out of scope of the AIAF.

Choose the option that best describes what you are doing. If more than one option applies, choose the one with the greatest potential impact.

How routing works
  • Personal, non-agency activity is outside AIAF scope, even when the tool is not agency-approved.
  • “AIAF not required” applies to approved, general-assistance use only when no organisational, sensitive or personal data, automation, operational decision support or autonomous action is involved.
  • Official workflows, decisions, services, systems and higher-impact uses proceed to the 16 core AIAF questions.
  • Assessing or buying the technology itself is not an AIAF use case; agency product-assurance processes remain separate.
  • A “Not sure” answer proceeds to the 16 core AIAF questions.
Question 1 Will this use case use an agency-approved AI platform, product or tool? Required

Approval requirements differ between agencies. This triage uses your answer only to determine whether the low-impact “AIAF not required” pathway can apply; it does not assess or replace your agency’s own processes.

Question 2 What are you using AI for? Required

Select the option that most closely describes the intended use—not only the technology being used.

Question 3 Does this use involve higher-impact activity or information? Required

Select Yes if the use could:

  • use organisational, sensitive or personal data;
  • be relied on by another person;
  • influence a decision about a person or organisation;
  • generate official or public-facing content;
  • change or automate a business process;
  • use real operational data or connect to a live agency system;
  • interact with external users; or
  • write code that may be deployed, trigger an action in a system or act autonomously.
